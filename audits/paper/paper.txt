6    Reordering guarded actions can change which terminal fault is observed and which effects become visible before
7    it. We isolate this interaction in a finite snapshot-action model: guards and primitive outcomes are functions
8    of entry inputs, disabled actions annul before outcome evaluation, emissions carry action identities, and faults
9    terminate the region under an explicitly declared observer. For a source order and a target ordered subset,
10
     we prove that semantic preservation is equivalent to five local fault-frontier conditions. The characterization
     connects full-permutation legality to established AND/OR precedence languages while exposing a separate
11
     action-selection boundary: unique acceptable fault representatives yield a unique least feasible set and
12
     polynomial positive-cost selection, whereas two interchangeable representatives per input make unit-cost
13
     branchless selection NP-complete. Completion and sparsification lemmas reduce exact search to representative
14   tuples without changing the semantics.
15       We instantiate the characterization in a canonical byte grammar for read-once entry-bit trees with private
16   leaves. A bounded optimizer combines exact leaf search with tree dynamic programming, and an independent
17   checker decodes the actual object, replays inherited guards and frontier witnesses, and enforces an external
18   byte budget. Handwritten proofs are accompanied by 1,061,510 direct semantic comparisons, 45,885 certificate
19   comparisons, 130 independently enumerated tree optima, 384 cross-method optimum comparisons, and 30
20   directed controls, with no recorded disagreement in the declared campaign. An observer-refinement family
21
     has an exact coarse optimum of 12 bytes and precise-origin optimum of 8 · 2𝑏 + 4 bytes in the grammar. The
     result is deliberately fragment-relative: it is not a production compiler, a mechanized general proof, a real-ISA
22
     size model, or a processor-performance claim.

33   1   Introduction
34
     A branch that prevents an operation from executing can also prevent that operation from raising an
35
     exception. Replacing branches with predicates, moving guarded operations, and later reintroducing
36
     branches therefore affects more than data dependences. It determines the first terminal fault and the
37
     prefix of visible effects that precedes that fault. A code-size budget adds a second, representation-
38
     sensitive obligation: a semantically legal transformation can still be inadmissible because a branch,
39
     an inherited guard, or a duplicated action occupies bytes in the declared target language.
40
        This interaction is easy to under-specify. “Preserve exceptions” might mean preserving only an
41
     exception class, preserving a complete fault payload, or preserving the identity of the operation that
42
     raised it. Similarly, “minimize code size” is meaningless until the target grammar states whether
43
     predicates may be strengthened, whether primitive operations may be synthesized, whether suffixes
44
     may be shared, and which bytes are charged. Two transformations can be compared only after
45
     both the observer and the representation have been frozen.
46

71      Predicated execution and if-conversion have a long compiler and architecture lineage. Hy-
72   perblocks, predicate analysis, speculative guarded execution, partial reverse if-conversion, and
73   branch-prediction trade-offs establish that control-to-data conversion interacts with liveness, code
74   growth, processor support, and scheduling [2, 14, 34–36, 53]. In particular, August, Hwu, and
75   Mahlke combine early aggressive if-conversion with scheduling-time partial reverse if-conversion
76   and evaluate a modeled architecture with nontrapping instruction variants [5]. That framework
77   motivates the problem but does not provide the finite terminal-observation theorem, selected-action
78   complexity boundary, or actual-byte certificate studied here.
79      We deliberately study a smaller interface. Every action’s guard and primitive outcome are fixed
80   by entry input. Outcomes are silent, uniquely tagged emissions, or terminal faults. The source
81   supplies a full order for each input; a target leaf supplies an ordered subset of inherited actions.
82   This snapshot abstraction removes mutable dataflow and handler semantics so that the interaction
83   among selection, order, cancellation, and observation can be characterized exactly. It assumes that
84   some external analysis has already justified the abstraction. General SSA extraction, memory effects,
85   undefined behavior, and a real exception ABI remain outside the result.
86      The central theorem gives necessary and sufficient fault-frontier conditions. A valid target retains
87   the source’s visible emission prefix in order, keeps selected faults after that prefix, selects at least
88   one acceptable terminal fault, and places an acceptable representative before every selected extra
89   emission or wrong-signature fault. One representative per input is enough to certify all cancellation
90   obligations. This turns semantic equivalence into a compact checker interface without pretending
91   that the checker establishes the correctness of compiler-to-model extraction.
92      The characterization also separates established scheduling machinery from the new semantic
93   boundary. With a fixed full action set, the derived obligations are exactly finite AND/OR prece-
94   dence conditions, and the known ready algorithm decides feasibility [37]. We do not claim a new
95   scheduling algorithm. Instead, we prove how first-fault semantics induces those conditions and
96   show what changes when the target may delete actions. If every faulting input has a unique ac-
97   ceptable representative, a unique least selected set exists. If each input may choose between two

130   2     Observation and Transformation Model
131   The model is intentionally finite and extensional. Its purpose is not to replace a compiler IR, but to
132   make every semantic premise used by the theorem explicit and replayable.

134   2.1    Inputs, actions, guards, and outcomes
135   Let A be a finite set of action identities and let X be a nonempty finite set of entry inputs. The
136   executable uses 𝑏 entry bits and therefore |X| = 2𝑏 , but the semantic results need only finiteness.
137   For every pair (𝑥, 𝑎) ∈ X × A, the model supplies a Boolean guard 𝑔(𝑥, 𝑎) and a raw outcome
138
                                       𝑟 (𝑥, 𝑎) ∈ {𝜏 } ∪ {emit(𝑎, 𝑣)} ∪ {fault(𝜅)}.
139
140   The payload 𝑣 and complete observer-visible fault signature 𝜅 may depend on 𝑥 and 𝑎, but they
141   are immutable functions of the entry input. Emission identities are unique: two different actions
142   cannot generate the same tagged event. This identity tag is what makes source-prefix actions
143   non-substitutable in the main theorem.
144     A disabled action is annulled before its raw outcome is evaluated. Thus a false guard neither
145   emits nor faults. An enabled silent action has no observation. An enabled emission appends its
146   tagged event and execution continues. An enabled fault appends its terminal signature and stops

148   the whole region. There is no handler, resumption, finally block, or secondary observation after the
149   fault. Normal termination has no additional return payload; any live output, external side effect, or
150   observed control event must be represented as a tagged emission.
151      These choices match the narrow phenomenon under study. They do not claim that guard
152   evaluation is generally pure, that all hardware faults are terminal, or that a compiler can infer the
153   table without proof. A memory load after a store, a trapping guard expression, an operation whose
154   floating-point status flags are observable, or an instruction in a language with caught exceptions
155   requires a richer semantics.

157   2.2   Source and target executions
158   For each input 𝑥, the source supplies a full permutation 𝜎𝑥 of A. Executing an ordered list means
159   visiting its actions left to right, annulling disabled actions and stopping at the first enabled fault.
160   The source’s suffix after that fault is latent: it appears in the permutation but is not executed.
161      A target leaf supplies a nonrepeating ordered subset 𝜋 of A. It may delete actions but may not
162   invent an action, strengthen a guard, alter an outcome, or repeat an identity. The target object is a
163   read-once binary decision tree over entry bits. Each branch tests a bit not previously tested on that
164   root-to-leaf path; each leaf owns its action sequence. Child subtrees and leaf sequences are private:
165   the grammar has no shared suffixes or DAG nodes.
166      For any ordered subset 𝜌, define Obs𝑥 (𝜌) as the word of tagged emissions followed by either
167   normal return or the complete signature of its first enabled fault. A target leaf is valid on a cell
168   𝐶 ⊆ X when

170   A target tree is valid when the reached leaf is valid for every input. This is a total finite-table
171   equality, not a probabilistic or sampled specification.

213   2.5    Why the restrictions are necessary
214
      Four short counterexamples delimit the theorem. First, if emissions are not identity tagged, one
215
      action can replace another equal-valued emission, so retaining every source-prefix identity is no
216
      longer necessary. Second, if evaluating a false guard can fault, annulling before the raw outcome is
217
      unsound. Third, if outcomes depend on mutable state, reordering can change a later action’s value
218
      even when the precomputed table says otherwise. Fourth, if a fault is caught, an emission in the
219
      source suffix can become visible and “first terminal fault” no longer determines the observation.
220
        These are not defects that can be repaired by adding another frontier edge. They change the
221
      semantic object. A production instantiation would need a source-language and IR-specific extraction
222
      theorem analogous to the semantic foundations used in verified or bounded translation validators
223
      [28, 29, 31, 32, 58]. The present work begins after that extraction boundary.

286   3.1 From observations to constraints
287   The theorem yields three kinds of order obligations. Adjacent prefix actions give ordinary edges.
288   The last prefix action precedes each selected fault, also an ordinary edge. Each barrier 𝑣 receives
289   one disjunctive condition (𝐺𝑥 ∩ 𝑆, 𝑣) requiring some acceptable representative before it. Coverage
290   adds a selection obligation 𝑆 ∩ 𝐺𝑥 ≠ ∅.
291     This separation matters for both correctness and complexity. If all good sets are singletons,
292   every disjunction collapses to an edge. If a good set has alternatives, forcing every member before
293   the barrier is sound but incomplete: it can reject legal orders such as 𝑎𝑧𝑏 in Figure 1. Conversely,
294

295   replacing the disjunction with no edge accepts 𝑧𝑎𝑏. The constraint language must preserve the

297     The source summary can be computed by direct execution of 𝜎𝑥 , while 𝐹𝑥 and 𝑀𝑥 inspect all
298   inherited actions under 𝑥, including the latent suffix. Ignoring that suffix is unsound because a
299   moved action can become visible in the target. In particular, a wrong-signature latent fault is a
300   barrier even though the source never reaches it.

302   3.2    Checking versus authenticating the model
303   Theorem 3.2 is relative to the supplied finite table. A checker can recompute all five conditions
304   and direct observations without trusting optimizer summaries. It cannot determine whether a
305   compiler correctly extracted the table from LLVM, MLIR, a source language, or an exception ABI.
306   General compiler representations contain control/data dependences [11, 15], iterative dataflow
307   facts [23], and semantics for memory, poison, undefined behavior, or dialect-specific operations
308   [25, 26]. Those obligations are intentionally not hidden inside the certificate.
309      The distinction parallels translation validation: validate a particular transformation under explicit
310   source and target semantics rather than trust the optimizer implementation [41, 43]. However, the
311   present semantics is far smaller than production validators. Its advantage is that the exact first-fault
312   obligation can be isolated and independently enumerated; its limitation is that a real compiler front
313   end remains a separate proof or engineering task.

315   4     Scheduling Languages and Action Selection
316   The frontier theorem has two different algorithmic consequences. With a fixed full action set, it
317   induces a known tractable scheduling language. When the target may delete actions, choosing fault
318   representatives introduces a set-selection problem whose complexity depends on the observer.
319
320   4.1    Full permutations are exactly AND/OR precedence languages
321   A canonical waiting condition (𝑌 , 𝑗) has nonempty 𝑌 ⊆ A \ { 𝑗 } and requires at least one member
322   of 𝑌 to appear before 𝑗. Conditions are conjoined, while predecessor alternatives within 𝑌 are
323   disjoined. This is the AND/OR precedence formalism studied by Möhring, Skutella, and Stork [37].
324
325      Theorem 4.1 (Representation by waiting conditions). On full permutations of a fixed action
326   set, the legality languages of finite snapshot-action models are exactly the languages defined by finite
327   families of canonical waiting conditions.

332

474   entry bit not previously tested on its root-to-leaf path. A leaf contains a nonrepeating ordered subset
475   of inherited action identities. Each leaf and each subtree is private. The grammar excludes shared

      7.6   Threats to validity
816     Shared implementation assumptions. All executable paths share Python, JSON parsing, and some
817   model utilities. Agreement can miss a common parser or specification error. The handwritten
818   proofs and independently enumerated direct leaf oracle reduce but do not eliminate this risk. A
819   proof-assistant formalization or an independently implemented checker would provide stronger
820   assurance.
821
822      Extraction and observation adequacy. The largest external validity threat is the absent compiler-
823   to-table extractor. The model assumes pure entry-snapshot guards/outcomes and a complete visible
824   fault signature. A real language or ABI may expose state, origin, flags, priority, handler behavior, or
825   undefined behavior not represented here. Passing certificates cannot compensate for an inadequate
826   source model.
827
828      Finite breadth. Exhaustive checks cover bounded action and input dimensions; generated mod-
829   els are not production workloads. The campaign is appropriate for falsifying a small semantic
830   implementation, not for demonstrating prevalence, speedup, energy, register pressure, or prac-
831   tical code-size improvement. TACO compiler systems commonly support practical claims with
832   production infrastructure and broad workloads [9, 40, 44, 59]; this work makes no such claim.
833

      8      Related Work, Limitations, and Conclusion
859
      The closest bodies of work establish that predication, exact scheduling, translation validation, and
860
      certified sparse search are individually mature. The contribution claimed here is not any one of those
861
      ingredients. It is the precise conjunction, in a deliberately narrow semantics, of first-terminal-fault
862
      frontiers, selected representative complexity, and actual-byte certificate replay for one declared
863
      grammar.
864
865
866
      8.1     Predication, if-conversion, and control restructuring
867   Trace scheduling moved global compaction across basic-block boundaries and made likely paths
868   a compiler object [16]. Early highly concurrent and VLIW designs exposed architectural support
869   that motivated speculative and predicated compilation [13, 19, 46]. If-conversion formalized the
870   replacement of control dependences by data dependences [2]; hyperblocks provided an influential
871   compiler structure for predicated execution [36]. Subsequent work analyzed partial versus full
872   predication, branch-prediction effects, guarded speculation, predicate analysis, and predicate-aware
873   allocation [14, 17, 21, 34, 35, 53].
874      Park and Schlansker’s predicated-execution analysis and phi-predication study compiler repre-
875   sentations that retain more control structure or introduce selective predication [10, 42]. August,
876   Hwu, and Mahlke’s framework is the most direct historical anchor: it performs aggressive early
877   if-conversion and then partial reverse if-conversion while scheduling, accounting for predicate
878   flow, liveness, duplication, resources, performance, and static code growth [5]. Its modeled machine
879   provides nontrapping variants for most potentially excepting instructions. Our result does not
880   replace that framework. It removes machine scheduling and mutable dataflow to prove an exact
881   terminal-observation condition and a fragment-relative byte theorem.
882
      ACM Trans. Arch. Code Optim., Vol. 0, No. 0, Article 0. Publication date: September 2026.
      Fault-Frontier Certificates for Budgeted Guarded Rebalancing                                                       0:19


883      More recent control transformations broaden the operational scope. Partial control-flow lin-
884   earization preserves selected control while linearizing paths for vectorization [38]. PAVER exploits
885   partial SIMD parallelism with compiler transformations and masked execution [59]. MERIT melds
886   instructions from alternative paths and must make synthesized operations safe [30]. These systems
887   can insert, combine, or retarget operations beyond our inherited-action grammar. Their practi-
888   cal correctness questions therefore cannot be answered solely by a PFC1 certificate, while their
889   transformation power can evade our private-leaf lower bounds.
890      Architectural predication work also evaluates concerns that the abstract byte object cannot
891   address. Han, Ahn, and Choi analyze power-efficient CGRA predication with gate-level and energy-
892   delay evidence [18]. Real architectures must account for execution resources, speculation, exception
893   deferral, code placement, and branch behavior. The present study intentionally reports none of
894   those quantities.
895
896   8.2   Compiler representations and semantic validation
897   Program dependence graphs and SSA make control/data relationships explicit and support broad
898   optimization frameworks [11, 15]. Classical dataflow theory and compiler texts explain the fixed-
899   point, alias, liveness, and code-generation machinery omitted from the snapshot interface [1, 23, 39].
900   The functional interpretation of SSA clarifies why values and joins can be reasoned about composi-
901   tionally [4], while LLVM and MLIR provide extensible industrial intermediate representations with
902   much richer operation semantics [25, 26]. Our finite table is an input to analysis, not a substitute
903   for those representations or an extraction algorithm from them.
904      Translation validation shifts trust from an optimizer implementation to a validator for each
905   produced transformation [41, 43]. Loop-transformation and run-time validation work develops
906   proof obligations for structured optimizations [60]; CompCert mechanizes compiler correctness
907   and validator components in a proof assistant [28, 29, 52]. Vellvm formalizes LLVM IR for verified
908   transformations [58]. Alive and Alive2 validate peephole and bounded LLVM transformations while
909   handling semantic features far beyond our model [31, 32]. Equivalence-modulo-inputs and Csmith
910   attack compilers by generating or reducing tests rather than proving a specific transformation
911   [27, 55]. The artifact borrows their discipline of independent semantic comparison, but its bounded
912   tables do not become a production validator through testing.
913      Algebraic approaches validate control-flow transformations at a different abstraction. Kleene
914   algebra with tests supplies an equational foundation [24]; guarded KAT obtains efficient reasoning
915   for uninterpreted guarded programs [51]. CF-GKAT targets efficient validation of nonlocal control-
916   flow transformations, and later work studies decision procedures for broader GKAT variants [56, 57].
917   These results prevent a claim that finite replay or guarded decision procedures are new in general.
918   Our theorem instead specializes the observation to tagged prefixes plus a first terminal fault,
919   permits action deletion, and derives a representative-selection boundary not stated by those general
920   trace-equivalence frameworks.
921
922   8.3   Scheduling, code generation, and exact optimization
923   AND/OR precedence constraints and their ready algorithm are established scheduling theory [37].
924   Optimal instruction scheduling has been formulated with integer programming and enumera-
925   tion [50, 54]. Preallocation scheduling and alternative cost functions show that register-pressure
926   objectives can materially change generated code [48, 49]; ant-colony scheduling studies the qual-
927   ity/scalability trade-off against exact methods on real compiler targets [47]. Combinatorial register
928   allocation and scheduling integrates decisions whose separation can lose quality [33]. Our action
929   costs have no latency, resource, register, or spill semantics. We use exact scheduling only as a
930   bounded semantic oracle after fixing a selected set.
931
                                    ACM Trans. Arch. Code Optim., Vol. 0, No. 0, Article 0. Publication date: September 2026.
      0:20                                                                                             Anon.


932      Optimal decision trees are NP-hard in general [20]; induction algorithms and reduced decision
933   diagrams optimize different predictive or Boolean objectives and often permit sharing absent
934   from private leaves [8, 45]. CORELS produces certifiably optimal rule lists for an empirical-risk
935   objective [3]. These analogies are important negative novelty evidence: enumeration, dynamic
936   programming, and a small certificate are not by themselves new. Theorems 3.2, 4.3, 4.4, and 4.7
937   supply the semantics-specific reason that the chosen search space is complete.
938      Real code-size optimization adds instruction selection, encoding, branch displacement, layout, and
939   link-time effects [12]. Profile-guided tracing and dynamic optimization use execution frequencies
940   and runtime behavior to choose profitable regions [6, 7]. The PFC1 objective instead charges an
941   analysis grammar exactly and has no profile. It is appropriate for testing a theorem about observer
942   and representation declarations, but it cannot support a machine-code compaction or performance
943   claim.
944
945   8.4    TACO calibration and compiler search
946   TACO compiler papers connect semantic or analysis ideas to compiler infrastructure and generated-
947   code evidence: synchronous reactions extend SSA, TAFFO couples analyses with precision tuning,
948   and a JavaScript compiler evaluates end-to-end parallelization [9, 40, 44]. Phase-ordering systems
949   such as COBAYN, MiCOMP, and clustering-based selection use workloads and compiler integration
950   [? ? ? ]; broader autotuning work includes focused iterative optimization, self-tuning compilers,
951   OpenTuner, CompilerGym, and learned tensor schedules [? ? ? ? ? ]. Those papers calibrate the
952   evidence expected for production claims. Our exact finite oracle is instead evaluated against
953   semantic constructions, and the absent compiler extractor remains a significance limitation. The
954   defensible delta is the first-fault frontier, its representative-selection boundary, and actual-byte
955   replay for one fixed grammar, not a universal priority claim.
956
957   8.5    Limitations
958   No pass extracts the table from LLVM, MLIR, or source code, and no theorem covers mutable
959   memory, effectful guards, handlers, cleanup, loops, concurrency, undefined behavior, asynchronous
960   faults, or real trap priority. Correctness is relative to identity-tagged emissions and a declared
961   complete fault signature. The private-leaf grammar excludes strengthened predicates, synthesized
962   operations, shared suffixes, arbitrary CFGs, and real instruction encoding; the budget excludes the
963   model and certificate. Global optimality applies only to admitted complete search with positive
964   additive leaf costs. The proofs are handwritten and the Python paths share a trusted base. Exhaustive
965   bounded tests, controls, retained replay, and a direct oracle increase confidence but do not constitute
966   mechanization, workload breadth, independent review, or evidence of hardware benefit.
967
968   8.6    Conclusion
969   For finite entry-snapshot actions, equality of the tagged visible prefix and first terminal fault is
970   exactly characterized by fault frontiers. Full-set legality is an established AND/OR precedence lan-
971   guage; selectable actions have a sharp observer-dependent boundary between a least mandatory set
972   and NP-complete two-representative selection. Completion and sparsification justify representative
973   search. In a fixed private-leaf byte grammar, an exact bounded optimizer and independent checker
974   turn these facts into replayable safety and budget claims. The campaign found no disagreement
975   within its declared families, but remains finite evidence around handwritten proofs. More generally,
976   any exception-preserving size claim must freeze the observer, admissible transformations, and
977   charging rule: changing one can change validity, complexity, and optimum.
